Concern 01

What Phase 3 changes

SmartShop grows from customer signup and refrigerator supervision into a store application: maintain products, receive stock, scan a cart, identify an optional member, simulate payment, save the sale, award points, and issue a receipt.

The existing application factory, Flask blueprints, SQLAlchemy extension, runtime ownership, refrigerator MQTT, email alerts, Gmail replies, and fan controller were the foundation. Phase 3 extends that foundation; it did not introduce all of it from scratch.

Before

Signup stored a hashed password. Customer listing existed without the new administrator guard. Startup created missing tables. Refrigerator services started together.

After

Login/logout, private accounts, administrator roles, inventory, two reader workflows, transactional checkout, loyalty points, saved receipts, migrations, and independently switchable services.

Scope of this report. A review of the working tree against Git HEAD and the Phase 3 guide, including new untracked files. This is a logical explanation of the final changes, not a reconstruction of development order or a list of commits. The handoff records that no implementation commits were made.

Concern 03

The data model: intentions versus completed facts

New records and extensions
RecordMeaningWhy it exists
Customer extensionsUnique generated membership_number, nonnegative points, is_adminAccount identity, loyalty balance, explicit privileges.
ProductMetadata, unique UPC/EPC, integer price_cents, stock, archived flagThe current sellable product definition.
ReceptionProduct, positive quantity, dateHistory explaining stock additions.
MembershipCardCard UID → customer email; one active card per customerKeep physical cards separate from generated membership numbers.
CheckoutBrowser owner, status, JSON items/suppressed list, optional memberA shopping intention that can change or be abandoned.
PurchaseUnique checkout reference, total, points, recipient, language, delivery statusA completed sale and its receipt delivery record.
PurchaseItemProduct reference plus name, price, quantity snapshotsA historical fact unaffected by future product edits.
Checkout ≠ purchase. Seeing an item in a cart is not a sale. A checkout can exist without a purchase; a completed checkout has at most one purchase because checkout_id is unique on Purchase.

Relationships: a product has many receptions and purchase items; a customer has purchases and at most one linked card; a purchase contains item snapshots. Email remains the existing customer primary key.

Historical example: tea sold for $3.25 remains “Tea · $3.25” on its saved receipt even if the catalog later renames it and changes the price to $4.00.

Concern 04

Why startup now migrates

Previously, startup called db.create_all(). That creates absent tables but does not add columns to an existing customer table. Phase 3 calls migrate() so existing customers acquire membership numbers, points, and administrator flags while preserving their email keys, contact fields, and password hashes.

  1. Create the schema-version table if needed.
  2. Inspect an existing customer table; add missing columns.
  3. Generate membership numbers for legacy rows and enforce uniqueness.
  4. Apply dialect-specific legacy constraints, then create the new tables.
  5. Record version 1. Repeating the migration is supported.

SQLite uses validation triggers for constraints that cannot simply be added to a populated legacy table. MySQL can alter the membership column to NOT NULL, but its DDL is not atomic: a failed upgrade can leave partial schema changes. Back up and rehearse against a copy.

scripts.setup_shop also provisions an existing account as administrator and imports four verified products. The importer requires reader_verified=true, unique identifiers, and the suffix order 4965 / 4970 / 4968 / 4951. It refuses conflicting metadata. Repeating the same import does not duplicate products or add stock.

Verification flag ≠ device measurement. The importer trusts the operator’s attestation. It cannot prove that a JSON EPC was actually read from hardware. Suffixes alone cannot establish complete EPCs or their leading zeros.

Concern 05

Products, stock, identifiers, and money

Administrator-only pages now create, list, edit, and delete/archive products, look up UPCs, and record repeated stock receptions. Validation happens on the server, with database uniqueness and stock/price constraints behind it.

Identifiers are strings

UPC: 1–13 ASCII digits. EPC: 2–24 hexadecimal characters in complete bytes, normalized to uppercase. 001234 must retain its zeros. Membership UID rules are separate: 4, 7, or 10 bytes.

Prices are cents

Input uses Decimal, accepts positive prices up to 999999.99 with at most two decimal places, then stores integer cents. $3.25 becomes 325. The chosen policy has no tax calculation.

Stock rule: registration adds zero; a positive dated reception adds quantity; a completed sale subtracts one for each distinct product in its cart. Scans, review, failed payment, and cancelled payment do not decrement stock.

Reception date cannot be in the future. The service increments stock and inserts its reception record together. Products with reception or sale history are archived when deleted; unused products can be physically deleted. This protects the references explaining inventory and sales.

Concern 06

Three identities you must keep separate

Who is acting?
IdentityUsed forDoes not imply
Logged-in customerPrivate account/history and card enrollmentAutomatic membership attachment to the current sale
Browser owner (random session sid)Guest cart ownership and exclusive reader operationsA customer account or administrator privileges
Confirmed scanned memberPurchase association, points, receipt emailLogin or access to private account history

Signup now validates and normalizes input, logs the new customer in, and redirects to their account. Login checks the saved password hash. Logout clears authentication. Session rotation preserves language, creates a new browser owner, and cancels old reader operations.

Public signup always creates a non-administrator. The deliberate local CLI promotion grants admin access. Server decorators protect product administration, receptions, customer listing, and refrigerator threshold changes; hiding navigation alone would not enforce this.

Every POST/PUT/PATCH/DELETE requires a CSRF token, including polling POSTs that can mutate carts. Cart access checks the browser owner. Receipt access accepts the original checkout owner or the logged-in associated customer, returning 404 for other users.

Cookie settings add HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax, optional Secure, and a configured persistent secret. Dynamic responses use no-store; shared error pages render HTTP errors.

Concern 07

Product scanning: reusable tags, owned sessions

The confirmed four UHF tags identify reusable products, not four individual units that become permanently sold. Each distinct tag contributes one unit in a checkout. A later checkout can use that same tag while stock remains.

ProductReader wraps an adapter exposing start(), poll(), and stop(). It owns a 60-second operation with owner, purpose, ID, deadline, and observed tags. Registration capture and checkout compete for the same reader. A conflicting browser receives “Reader busy.”

The wrapper normalizes and deduplicates EPC observations. Checkout deduplicates by product ID too. Removing an item places it in suppressed, so repeated radio observations do not undo the user's choice; explicit restoration allows it back.

Implemented boundary, missing transport. FakeReader queues development EPCs. With fake mode off, UnavailableReader reports “CF600 SDK unavailable.” No native CF600 protocol or Pi compatibility was invented. Real scanning still needs the SDK and target-device evidence.

UPC keyboard-wedge handling accepts a scanner acting as a keyboard; Enter moves focus rather than accidentally submitting. Manual/keyboard behavior is implemented, but the actual barcode scanner model and printed UPCs remain unverified.

Read: reader wrapper · barcode/EPC UI · reader diagnostic

Concern 08

Membership scanning: intent before observation

A second device—ESP32 + RC522—reads membership card UIDs. It has a dedicated sketch and MQTT namespace, leaving refrigerator sketches intact. A generated membership number belongs to the account; a UID belongs to the physical card.

Arm operation→Remove / present card→Validate event→Explicitly confirm

Why arm first? A card observed near a shared reader should not silently attach itself to whichever browser happens to poll next. The browser requests a specific purpose, and the resulting observation must belong to that request.

Protocol protections and the problem each addresses
MechanismPurpose
One operation, owner, purpose, unique operation IDPrevent cross-browser or enrollment/checkout confusion.
60-second deadlineLimit the lifetime of unattended requests.
Reader epoch regenerated on reconnectInvalidate commands/events from a previous reader connection.
Heartbeat every 5 seconds; backend freshness limit 15 secondsInvalidate operations if the reader disappears.
Non-retained traffic; backend rejects retained messagesAvoid reusing broker-stored observations as fresh input.
First valid event onlyDuplicate delivery cannot select a second card.
Firmware requires absence then fresh presentation; NTP expiry checksA card already sitting on the reader cannot satisfy a newly armed request.

Default topics: SmartShop/membership/command (backend → reader), /status and /event (reader → backend). Commands use QoS 1; events/status use QoS 0. Firmware uses SS5, RST4, SCK18, MISO19, MOSI23, 3.3 V, and 115200-baud diagnostics.

Enrollment requires login, an available card, and confirmation. Linking a UID already owned by another customer fails. Replacing your card requires an explicit replacement choice. Unknown checkout cards allow retry or guest checkout; they never create an account automatically.

The backend supports configured MQTT credentials/TLS. Firmware currently uses plain LAN MQTT: deploy on the documented trusted LAN with broker ACLs, or add validated TLS for other networks. Operation IDs do not authenticate an untrusted publisher.

Concern 09

Follow one purchase from start to finish

  1. Start. Cancel this browser's active reader operations and abandon its earlier open carts. Create a new browser-owned Checkout.
  2. Scan. Arm the product reader. Polling resolves EPCs and updates the cart. Unknown, archived, and unavailable products produce feedback.
  3. Review. Reload product names/prices and availability, then stop product scanning. The cart is still not a sale.
  4. Choose loyalty association. Scan and explicitly confirm a linked card, or continue as a guest. Logging in alone has not chosen the member.
  5. Payment screen. Stop reader operations and change to payment state. Offer simulated success, failure, or cancellation; collect no real card credentials.
  6. Success. Finalize one database transaction, then attempt member receipt email. Redirect to the saved receipt.

Failure/cancellation at the payment screen returns to review with no sale deduction. Returning to scanning clears membership selection. A stock or reviewed-price mismatch blocks finalization and requires review again.

Read: cart(), cart_poll(), payment() · review() · cart polling

Concern 10

The transaction is the correctness boundary

The critical change is finalize(). Think of it as one sealed envelope containing every database effect of a sale. Either the whole envelope is accepted, or none of it is.

return existing purchase if already finalized
claim checkout only if status == payment
for each product in stable ID order:
    decrement stock only if available and reviewed name/price match
create Purchase + PurchaseItem snapshots
add member points, if confirmed
COMMIT
on any failure: ROLLBACK

Compare-and-set: the checkout update succeeds only when its current database status is payment. Together with the unique Purchase.checkout_id, this prevents repeated success submissions from charging inventory or points twice.

Conditional stock update: “subtract one where stock ≥ 1” makes the database arbitrate competing buyers. Checking stock in Python and subtracting later would leave a gap where two carts could both sell the last unit.

Last-unit example. Two carts review stock = 1. The first finalizes and stock becomes 0. The second conditional decrement changes no row, so its entire sale rolls back. No second purchase or points are saved. Its earlier review did not reserve inventory.

A process-wide checkout lock serializes related route workflows. Database conditions supply protection beyond that Python lock. Stable product update order also reduces conflicting lock acquisition in multi-item transactions.

Members earn floor(total CAD) points per purchase: total_cents // 100. For $8.75 that is 8 points. A guest earns 0. No redemption feature or real payment-provider integration is introduced.

Concern 12

Why email is outside the sale

The saved receipt is generated from Purchase and PurchaseItem data. Recipient and language are saved too. Member receipts go to the confirmed account's saved email, using a separate RECEIPT_FROM; ALERT_TO is for refrigerator alerts.

Resend delivery happens after the purchase commits. A network timeout cannot undo a paid sale. Instead, persisted receipt status tracks pending → sending → sent/failed, with not_required for guests.

Delivery conditionally claims an eligible attempt, increments attempts, sends with the stable key receipt-<purchase-id>, and saves the result. A failed attempt can be retried; a sending claim older than five minutes can be reclaimed. This is request-triggered delivery with explicit retry, not a new background email queue.

If email fails: keep the purchase, stock deduction, and points. Retry receipt delivery only. Re-running the sale to fix an email would create the wrong business action.

Provider idempotency helps with ambiguous responses, but it is not an unlimited exactly-once guarantee. The README calls for checking provider logs before retrying ambiguous timeouts outside the provider retention window.

Concern 13

Runtime and refrigerator continuity

Phase 3 adds membership/product readers and a checkout lock to Runtime. The old unconditional startup becomes independently switchable: fridge MQTT/cooldown, Gmail polling, and membership MQTT can each be enabled as needed. Checkout can run with fake product input and no devices.

Application construction remains free of device startup, worker startup, and database I/O. Cleanup attempts every service even if one adapter fails, joins worker threads, shuts down the alert executor, and releases the fan.

What survives a restart?
Database: survivesMemory: reset/rearm
Accounts, cards, products, receptions, carts, purchases, points, receipt statusReader leases, thresholds, readings, alert suppression, cooldown timers, connections, locks, software fan state

One serving process must own the physical fan and reader leases. Persistent carts do not make those in-memory leases safe across multiple processes.

The existing refrigerator control loop remains: an over-threshold reading sends an alert; a qualifying new YES Gmail reply activates the fan; both valid temperatures at/below threshold for five seconds stop it. Phase 3 protects threshold writes with administrator access and CSRF, and translates the dashboard; it does not replace this control loop.

Concern 14

Presentation and developer setup changes

New Jinja pages cover products, inventory, enrollment, cart, payment, receipt, account, and login. The base layout now changes navigation by authentication/role, supplies CSRF metadata, and offers persistent English/French selection.

Translations include server validation, notifications, scan status, HTTP error presentation, and receipt text. They change labels, not stored EPCs, UIDs, prices, or stock. CSS extends the responsive foundation, including stacked mobile cart/receipt rows, focus styles, labels, and scrollable tables.

Small JavaScript files poll cart changes, capture EPCs, perform UPC lookup, and refresh enrollment readiness. The backend remains authoritative; editing displayed prices cannot change the saved sale rules.

Configuration now permits DATABASE_URL or configurable MySQL host/name, persistent session secrets, cookie policy, broker port, membership credentials/TLS, service switches, and a separate receipt sender. requirements-pi.txt adds GPIO support. Ignore rules protect local secrets and keep the implementation guide shareable while source PDFs/photos remain local ignored assets.
